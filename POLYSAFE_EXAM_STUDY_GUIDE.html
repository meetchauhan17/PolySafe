Unit 1: Problem Formulation & Core Medical Urgency

CRITICAL METRIC TO MEMORIZE: Over 30% of emergency hospital admissions in adults aged 65 and older are caused by Adverse Drug Events (ADEs) resulting from polypharmacy. More than 50% of these hospitalizations are 100% clinically preventable with continuous cross-regimen screening.

Polypharmacy is clinically defined as taking 5 or more concurrent medications (Hyperpolypharmacy = 10+). Conventional drug checkers (such as 1mg, Practo, or WebMD) fail because they evaluate medications two-at-a-time in static isolation. PolySafe addresses the 4 fundamental clinical failure modes:

Clinical Failure Mode Why Conventional Systems Fail PolySafe Precision Solution
1. Specialist Silos Cardiologists, orthopedics, and neurologists prescribe independently with zero mutual regimen visibility. Provider Swimlane Timeline: Consent-based 6-digit PIN and QR sync unifying all specialists into one view.
2. Prescribing Cascades A drug side-effect is misdiagnosed as a new condition, triggering a second unnecessary prescription. Cascade Correlator: Chronologically maps symptom onset against 581 validated adverse drug side-effect profiles.
3. Indian Brand Masking 70%+ of Indian drugs are Fixed-Dose Combinations (FDCs) with brand names hiding multiple chemical salts. 5-Layer Salt Resolver: Decomposes trade brands (e.g., Combiflam) into constituent salts (Ibuprofen + Paracetamol).
4. Herbal Blind Spots Patients omit supplements thinking "herbal means safe", triggering fatal bleeding or enzyme inhibition. Botanical Matrix: 526 clinical monographs cross-referencing herb-drug interactions (e.g., Warfarin + Ginkgo).

Unit 2: The 5 Deterministic Safety Subsystems

EXAM KEY POINT: PolySafe’s risk calculations are 100% deterministic, not generative. The AI LLM (Groq LLaMA 3.3 70B) is strictly used as an explanation translator, never to fabricate medical severity tiers or clinical rules.

1. 5-Layer Indian Formulation Resolver

Converts trade names into active molecules:

  • L1: Curated Indian Brand Salt Knowledge Base (Augmentin 625, Zerodol SP, Pan-D).
  • L2: Direct NLM RxNorm API lookup.
  • L3: RxNorm Approximate String Matcher (catches typos like "Metforrmin").
  • L4: Botanical Supplement Matrix (Ashwagandha, Ginkgo, Turmeric).
  • L5: AI Extraction Fallback via Groq LLaMA with chemistry validation.

2. WHO 5-Tier Cumulative Regimen Engine

Calculates cumulative risk over 222,383 verified DDInter records:

  • L1 Minimal: Standard monitoring.
  • L2 Low: Food/spacing guidance.
  • L3 Moderate: Dosage adjustment suggested.
  • L4 High: Significant risk; physician consult needed.
  • L5 Critical: Severe contraindication (e.g. Warfarin + Aspirin).

3. Prescribing Cascade Correlator

Evaluates chronological drug additions against 581 documented cascade rules. Example: If a patient logs "swollen ankles" within 30 days of starting Amlodipine, it flags that this is an adverse effect—warning doctors against wrongly prescribing Furosemide.

4. Anticholinergic Cognitive Burden (ACB)

Uses the validated Boustani Scale (567 calibrated drug scores). In geriatric patients, an ACB Score ≥ 3 triples the risk of memory impairment, confusion, delirium, falls, and early dementia.

5. Multi-Modal Intelligent Intake

Dual vision pipeline: Google Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite handles complex handwriting + Tesseract OCR provides offline fallback. Loose pills are identified via FDA/NLM stamp code search (e.g. imprint L484 = Acetaminophen 500mg).

Unit 5: Judge Viva Voce Defense Bible (Top 15 Q&A)

Q1 How is this different from 1mg, Practo, or WebMD drug interaction tools?
Answer: Existing tools only test pairwise (A + B) in isolation, giving patients 15 confusing alerts with zero prioritization. They completely miss cumulative multi-drug toxicity (e.g., three mild sedatives combining into respiratory depression), they lack Anticholinergic Cognitive Burden calculations for geriatric dementia risks, they don't detect Prescribing Cascades, and they have no Doctor Pre-Prescription Simulator uniting multiple specialists.
Q2 What if the prescription OCR misreads handwriting and adds the wrong drug?
Answer: We enforce a Human-in-the-Loop review pipeline. Extracted medicines from Gemini and Tesseract are staged on an interactive confirmation card showing confidence scores. The patient, doctor, or pharmacist must explicitly verify and approve the drug name and dosage before it is committed to the database.
Q3 How can doctors trust the AI? Aren't LLMs prone to medical hallucinations?
Answer: Our safety checks are 100% deterministic and rule-based, powered by verified clinical databases (DDInter, NLM RxNorm, and Boustani scales). The LLM is restricted to synthesizing human-readable explanations from pre-computed clinical facts; it is never allowed to invent severity levels or interaction pairs.
Q4 How does PolySafe handle Indian Fixed-Dose Combinations (FDCs)?
Answer: Via our 5-Layer Indian Formulation Resolver. Trade names like Combiflam, Augmentin 625, or Zerodol-SP are mapped to their constituent chemical salts (e.g. Aceclofenac + Paracetamol). Interactions are evaluated at the molecular constituent level rather than the trade name level.
Q5 How do you protect patient privacy between doctors and caregivers?
Answer: We enforce strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC). Caregivers only see adherence tracking (taken vs. missed doses) and reminders; they cannot view private clinical diagnoses. Doctor access is ephemeral and strictly consent-based through a time-limited 6-digit PIN and QR code generated by the patient.
Q6 Can PolySafe work in rural clinics with spotty or no internet?
Answer: Yes. PolySafe is built as an installable Progressive Web App (PWA) with service worker caching. The core medicine schedule, local emergency medical card, and offline fallback databases remain accessible even when the device is completely disconnected.
Q7 Why do you include herbal supplements like Ashwagandha or Ginkgo Biloba?
Answer: Over 60% of patients do not disclose herbal supplements to their doctors. Supplements are biologically active: Ginkgo Biloba and high-dose Turmeric potently inhibit platelets, causing fatal hemorrhages when combined with anticoagulants like Warfarin. Our 526-monograph botanical matrix closes this dangerous blind spot.
Q8 What is an Anticholinergic Cognitive Burden (ACB) and why is it dangerous?
Answer: Many common medications (allergy, bladder, sleep, nerve pain) block acetylcholine in the central nervous system. When multiple low-scoring drugs combine to reach an ACB score ≥ 3, elderly patients experience confusion, hallucinations, sedation, and a 3x higher incidence of catastrophic fall fractures.
Q9 What is a 'Prescribing Cascade' and how does your engine detect it?
Answer: A prescribing cascade happens when an adverse drug reaction is misinterpreted as a new disease, leading to unnecessary additional prescriptions (e.g., Amlodipine causes ankle edema → doctor prescribes Furosemide). PolySafe tracks drug start dates chronologically and correlates newly logged symptoms against 581 clinical side-effect profiles.
Q10 What database and hosting infrastructure does PolySafe utilize?
Answer: We use Neon Serverless Postgres connected through Prisma ORM with connection pooling, hosted on Node 22 / Express 5 on Vercel Serverless. This provides auto-scaling compute, instant branch replication, zero-idle cost, and enterprise security.
Q11 How do you prevent brute force attacks on authentication?
Answer: Three layers of defense: IP rate-limiting (50 attempts per 15 min), email-specific OTP rate limits (5 per hour), and automatic per-account lockouts after 5 consecutive failed logins, evaluated with timing-attack-safe bcrypt verification.
Q12 How do you prove this system is technically verified?
Answer: We maintain an automated 18-Step System & API Audit Suite (npm test) that runs through end-to-end user lifecycles, drug additions, DDInter flags, cascade matches, doctor QR pairing, and simulator runs. All 18 steps pass with 100% success.
Q13 What is the Emergency Medical Passport?
Answer: A one-click, printable and scannable summary containing the patient's active drug list, severe allergies, blood group, emergency contacts, and an EMT-scannable QR code formatted for instantaneous triage during ambulance or ER emergencies.
Q14 Can a doctor override a PolySafe warning?
Answer: Yes. PolySafe is a Clinical Decision Support System (CDSS), not a prescriptive blocker. If an oncologist or cardiologist intentionally combines two interacting drugs under close inpatient observation, they can acknowledge the alert and document clinical rationale.
Q15 What is your future commercialization and technical roadmap?
Answer: (1) Integration with hospital Electronic Health Records (EHR) via FHIR / HL7 APIs; (2) Wearable integration with Apple Health and Google Fit to monitor vitals during drug onboarding; and (3) Pharmacogenomic (PGx) profile integration to account for genetic metabolic variations.
