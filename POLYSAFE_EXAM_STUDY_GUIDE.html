2. Mathematical Formulations & Deterministic Scoring Algorithms

CORE ARCHITECTURAL AXIOM: In clinical safety, deterministic mathematics must always supersede generative heuristics. All numerical risk tiers, gauges, and safety banners are calculated strictly by pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic equations, validated odds ratios, and deterministic graph traversals.

2.1 Regimen Cumulative Risk Index Formula (WHO / NCI 5-Tier Model)

Let $\mathcal{M} = \{m_1, m_2, \dots, m_N\}$ be the set of $N$ active medications in the patient's regimen. Each drug $m_i$ is mapped to an inherent harm tier $h_i = \text{HarmLevel}(m_i) \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$:

1. Regimen Mean Toxicity:   μrisk = (1 / N) ∑i=1..N hi
2. Inherent Worst-Case Toxicity:   hmax = max1 ≤ i ≤ N (hi)
3. Major Flag Count:   Fmajor = |{ f ∈ ℱ ∣ Severity(f) ∈ {MAJOR, CONTRAINDICATED} }|

The final unified regimen risk tier $\mathcal{L}_{\text{regimen}} \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$ is evaluated through a clinical prioritization decision tree:

Decision Tree:
• Level 5 (CRITICAL):   If   hmax = 5   OR   Factive ≥ 3
• Level 4 (HIGH):   Else if   μrisk ≥ 3.5   OR   Fmajor ≥ 1
• Level 3 (MODERATE):   Else if   μrisk ≥ 2.5
• Level 2 (MILD):   Else if   μrisk ≥ 1.5
• Level 1 (LOW):   Otherwise (μrisk < 1.5)
CLINICAL REASONING: If a patient takes even a single Level 5 critical narrow-therapeutic-index drug (e.g. Warfarin or Digoxin), $h_{\max} = 5$ forces the regimen into Level 5 (Critical) immediately. Harmless multivitamins cannot dilute acute organ-failure liabilities.

2.2 Anticholinergic Cognitive Burden (ACB) Summation & Risk Stratification

Anticholinergic medications competitively block muscarinic acetylcholine receptors ($M_1 - M_5$). In geriatric patients, diminished central cholinergic reserve transforms mild drug burdens into acute delirium, falls, and dementia.

Cumulative Cognitive Burden:   TotalACB = ∑i=1..N Score(mi)
where each drug score is assigned from the validated Boustani Scale: Score(mi) ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3}.
Total ACB Score Clinical Stratification Pharmacodynamic Impact on Geriatric Cohort
0 None (Normal) No demonstrable central muscarinic blockade; baseline cognitive integrity preserved.
1 – 2 Moderate Burden Mild psychomotor slowing, daytime drowsiness; requires vigilance in ambulation.
≥ 3 CRITICAL BURDEN Triples the risk of delirium, hallucinations, memory decline, and fall-related fractures.

2.3 DDInter Pairwise Severity Weighting & Matrix Traversal

Given $N$ active medications, the system evaluates all combination pairs $\binom{N}{2} = \frac{N(N-1)}{2}$ across 222,383 verified clinical pairs. Each pair is ranked by numerical severity weight $W_k$:

Weight Scale:   WContraindicated = 4  >  WMajor = 3  >  WModerate = 2  >  WMinor = 1

2.4 Duplicate Active Ingredient Toxicity & Safe Ceiling Limits

When multiple Fixed-Dose Combinations share an identical chemical salt $S_k$, cumulative daily dosage is calculated across all sources:

Dosecumulative(Sk) = ∑i ∈ Products Dose(mi, Sk) × DailyFrequency(mi)
Alert Trigger:   Dosecumulative(Sk) > CeilingLimit(Sk)
Example: Paracetamol ceiling is 4,000 mg/day (2,000 mg in hepatic impairment). Taking Combiflam (325mg × 3) + Calpol 650 (650mg × 4) = 3,575 mg, approaching hepatic saturation.

2.5 Prescribing Cascade Directed Graph Traversal

A prescribing cascade is modeled as a directed temporal graph $G = (V, E)$, where $V = \text{Drugs} \cup \text{Symptoms}$:

Cascade Condition:   (mA → s) ∈ E  ∧  (s → mB) ∈ E  ∧  t(mA) < t(s) ≤ t(mB)
Clinical Logic: Drug A was started; symptom $s$ appeared afterward; Drug B was prescribed to treat $s$. PolySafe flags $s$ as a known adverse event of $m_A$, instructing the clinician to discontinue $m_A$ rather than continuing $m_B$.

To provide specialists with immediate organ clearance insights, each regimen generates a 4-dimensional toxicity vector:

3. PolySafe Solution Architecture: The 5 Core Subsystems

Subsystem 1: 5-Layer Indian Formulation Resolver

Resolves regional trade names to standardized molecular salts:

  • Layer 1 (Indian FDC Base): Resolves branded combinations like Augmentin 625 (Amoxicillin 500mg + Clavulanate 125mg).
  • Layer 2 (NLM RxNorm API): International standard RxCUI code resolution.
  • Layer 3 (Approximate Matching): Handles misspellings via Levenshtein / trigram distance.
  • Layer 4 (Botanical Library): Maps Ayurvedic & herbal supplements (Ashwagandha, Ginkgo, Curcumin).
  • Layer 5 (LLM Fallback): Groq LLaMA extraction verified against chemical databases.

Subsystem 2: Deterministic Regimen Engine

Continuous WHO 5-Tier status calculation across 222k+ DDInter records, evaluating drug-drug, herb-drug, and disease-drug contraindications (Beers 2023 & STOPP/START v3).

Subsystem 3: Prescribing Cascade Correlator

Reverse-timeline engine matching newly logged symptoms against 581 clinical side-effect profiles to halt misdiagnosis chains.

Subsystem 4: Anticholinergic Cognitive Burden Meter

Computes cumulative Boustani score and renders a real-time risk gauge, alerting doctors to dementia and delirium liabilities.

Subsystem 5: Multimodal Intelligent Intake

Dual vision OCR pipeline (Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite for handwriting + Tesseract OCR for offline fallback) plus FDA/NLM Pill Imprint stamp identifier (e.g. code L484 = Acetaminophen 500mg).

Q1 How is this different from 1mg, Practo, or WebMD drug interaction tools?
Answer: Existing commercial tools only test pairwise (A + B) in isolation, giving patients 15 confusing alerts with zero prioritization. They completely miss cumulative multi-drug toxicity (e.g. three mild sedatives combining into respiratory depression), lack Anticholinergic Cognitive Burden calculations for geriatric dementia risks, do not detect Prescribing Cascades, and provide no Doctor Pre-Prescription Simulator uniting multiple specialists.
Q2 What if the prescription OCR misreads handwriting and adds the wrong drug?
Answer: We enforce a Human-in-the-Loop review pipeline. Extracted medicines from Gemini and Tesseract are staged on an interactive confirmation card showing confidence scores. The patient, doctor, or pharmacist must explicitly verify and approve the drug name and dosage before it is committed to the database.
Q3 How can doctors trust the AI? Aren't LLMs prone to medical hallucinations?
Answer: Our safety checks are 100% deterministic and rule-based, powered by verified clinical databases (DDInter, NLM RxNorm, and Boustani scales). The LLM is restricted to synthesizing human-readable explanations from pre-computed clinical facts; it is never allowed to invent severity levels or interaction pairs.
Q4 How does PolySafe handle Indian Fixed-Dose Combinations (FDCs)?
Answer: Via our 5-Layer Indian Formulation Resolver. Trade names like Combiflam, Augmentin 625, or Zerodol-SP are mapped to their constituent chemical salts (e.g. Aceclofenac + Paracetamol). Interactions are evaluated at the molecular constituent level rather than the trade name level.
Q5 How do you protect patient privacy between doctors and caregivers?
Answer: We enforce strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC). Caregivers only see adherence tracking (taken vs. missed doses) and reminders; they cannot view private clinical diagnoses. Doctor access is ephemeral and strictly consent-based through a time-limited 6-digit PIN and QR code generated by the patient.
Q6 Can PolySafe work in rural clinics with spotty or no internet?
Answer: Yes. PolySafe is built as an installable Progressive Web App (PWA) with service worker caching. The core medicine schedule, local emergency medical card, and offline fallback databases remain accessible even when the device is completely disconnected.
Q7 Why do you include herbal supplements like Ashwagandha or Ginkgo Biloba?
Answer: Over 60% of patients do not disclose herbal supplements to their doctors. Supplements are biologically active: Ginkgo Biloba and high-dose Turmeric potently inhibit platelets, causing fatal hemorrhages when combined with anticoagulants like Warfarin. Our 526-monograph botanical matrix closes this dangerous blind spot.
Q8 What is an Anticholinergic Cognitive Burden (ACB) and why is it dangerous?
Answer: Many common medications (allergy, bladder, sleep, nerve pain) block acetylcholine in the central nervous system. When multiple low-scoring drugs combine to reach an ACB score ≥ 3, elderly patients experience confusion, hallucinations, sedation, and a 3x higher incidence of catastrophic fall fractures.
Q9 What is a 'Prescribing Cascade' and how does your engine detect it?
Answer: A prescribing cascade happens when an adverse drug reaction is misinterpreted as a new disease, leading to unnecessary additional prescriptions (e.g., Amlodipine causes ankle edema → doctor prescribes Furosemide). PolySafe tracks drug start dates chronologically and correlates newly logged symptoms against 581 clinical side-effect profiles.
